Where this starts

Aerial inspection ends at the top of the module.Everything that fails starts underneath it.

01

The fault is invisible from above

A drone captures the top of the glass. Every terminal, every MC4, every junction box lives on the underside — the one surface no aerial pass, and no walk down the access road, ever actually sees.

02

The clock is the money

The modules were bought directly and issued to PCL to install, so a hot connector is JA Solar's or Rizen's problem to fix — while the evidentiary bar for proving it is high enough that a phone photo from a walk-down rarely clears it. That clock runs from commissioning, not from whenever someone gets around to raising the claim.

03

One pass beats a campaign

The site's current process is a handheld Fluke XV and a work-order queue, run when someone has time. A unit that inspects on every pass produces the same reference points every time — a comparable, dated, geolocated record instead of a single snapshot nobody can reproduce.

The obligation

In New South Wales, a hot DC connection is a work-health-and-safety matter, not just a warranty one.

The Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW) and AS/NZS 5033 require DC wiring and connectors to be kept safe and inspected by licensed personnel — exactly the fault class this proposal targets. SafeWork NSW has run compliance checks at NSW solar farms since 2021, naming DC faults and fire risk explicitly.

Neither sets a threshold or an interval. The standard is whatever you can evidence — a dated, geolocated record of what was found, where, and when. Every pass leaves one automatically.

The path

PCU-26 first. Then the standard travels with the asset.

01

PCU-26, five days

One inverter block, ~6 MWp, ~1.2% of Stubbo — demarcated in your own as-built drawing STSP-SMEC-EL-00ELE-DWG-126 Rev 05. Onboarding and a single thermal-and-visual pass across all 24 combiner circuits, CBX-26-1-1 through CBX-26-4-5. A$10,000 fixed, excluding GST, plus mobilisation at documented cost + 10% (estimated A$4,500) — 50% on execution, 50% on commissioning completion.

02

What you get

A geolocated findings report referenced to your own CBX and string identifiers, MC4 and loom heating reported as its own section so it can be lodged against JA Solar and Rizen without being unpicked from the rest, and Aptics Console access for the trial period plus 30 days.

03

The whole site

520 MWp, phased by inverter block, at A$950 per MWp (DC) per year — four whole-site passes annually, a 3-year term with 5% escalation. The trial fee is credited in full against onboarding if you proceed within 30 days of the report.
